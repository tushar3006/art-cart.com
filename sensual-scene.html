Candlelight

A scene in one evening

The rain had settled into something soft, a steady murmur against the window, as if the city outside had agreed to lower its voice. Inside, the only light came from the candles she had set along the sill, and each one threw a small, trembling gold onto the walls.

He stood at the counter, sleeves rolled to the elbow, pouring the last of the wine. She watched his hands. They were unhurried and sure, and he never looked up, though he knew she was watching. That was part of it. They had both learned how much a held glance could carry.

"You're quiet," he said.

"I'm listening to the rain." She smiled and tucked a strand of hair behind her ear. "And to you not asking me to say it."

He crossed the room slowly and handed her the glass. Their fingers met around the stem and stayed there a moment longer than the gesture needed. Neither of them moved away. The warmth of his hand traveled up her wrist, and the room seemed to narrow until it held only the two of them and the small sounds between them: the rain, the hush of the candles, one slow breath.

"Dance with me," she said.

"There's no music."

"Then we'll have to make our own."

So they did. His hand settled at the small of her back and hers rested against his chest, where she could feel his heartbeat keeping a time that wasn't quite steady. They turned slowly across the old wooden floor, and the candlelight stretched their shadows long and tangled against the wall until it was hard to say where one ended and the other began.

She felt his breath at her temple. She felt, more than heard, the low laugh in his chest when she leaned in closer. This, she thought. This is the part I'd keep, if I could keep only one.

When the dance ran out of steps they simply stood there, foreheads touching, eyes closed, waiting. The wanting was a living thing between them, patient and warm, and neither of them was in any hurry to end it.

Then he kissed her, slowly, the way you open a letter you've waited a long time to read. She answered it with her whole self, her fingers curling into his shirt, and the world outside the window fell away.

One candle guttered and went out. Nobody noticed.

The rain kept on, and the rest of the night belonged to them alone.
